# Measured claims

Which claims from the EuroVis submission survive re-measurement with the
corrected pipeline, and which do not. Generated by `npm run bench:all`; the
underlying numbers are in `benchmarks/out/results.csv`.

Measurement conditions: 800x500 canvas for every library, identical curve
flattening (0.25 px), 2x2 rasterization samples per pixel. `flex-sankey` run
with `nodeAlign: "center"`, `whitespace: 0.3`, unpacked paths, 40 seeded
ordering passes; the comparison libraries at their own defaults.

## Survives, and more strongly than claimed

**Fewer crossings than `d3-sankey-diagram`.** The submission reported winning
9 of 10 tests, around 10% better on average. Re-measured, `flex-sankey` wins
**20 of 20** datasets across both suites.

| Suite | d3-sankey | d3-sankey-diagram | flex-sankey |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| energy | 148.5 | 122.6 | **112.0** |
| layered | 911.7 | 745.5 | **622.2** |

**About three times less overlap than `d3-sankey`.** Reported as "about 3 times
as much overlap on average"; measured at 3.08x on the energy suite. That
library routes links directly from source to target without dummy nodes, so
its ribbons cross freely over intervening nodes.

**Path length ordering.** `d3-sankey` shortest, `flex-sankey` second,
`d3-sankey-diagram` longest — exactly as described. The submission put
`d3-sankey-diagram` 13% longer than ours; measured at 9%.

**"The number of crossings dropped from 122 to 73" with totalizing nodes.**
Replicates. Over six datasets of the energy suite, totalizing nodes reduce
crossings from 113 to 61, a 46% drop against the submission's reported 40%.
Overlap falls with it, from 6.5% to 2.5%.

The count is the same whether crossings are counted topologically, as
inversions between adjacent layers, or geometrically, as intersections between
the drawn ribbons: the two agree exactly on every dataset measured, with
totalizing nodes and without. That agreement between two independent
implementations is a useful mutual check on both.

## Does not survive

**"`d3-sankey-diagram` produces diagrams with 68% more overlap on average."**
It does not. The two are statistically indistinguishable:

| Suite | d3-sankey-diagram | flex-sankey | difference |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| energy | 7.29% | 7.18% | 1.5% |
| layered | 14.37% | 14.36% | 0.1% |

Per dataset, `flex-sankey` has the lower overlap on 9 of 20 and the higher on
11. The claimed gap appears to have been an artefact of the original
measurement pipeline rather than a property of the layouts. Three defects in
that pipeline, each now fixed and separately tested, would all have skewed this
figure:

- circular arcs were reduced to their chords, losing about 36% of a
  semicircle's length, and `d3-sankey-diagram` is the one compared library that
  draws with arcs;
- coverage depth was inferred from alpha compositing at 50% opacity, which
  cannot distinguish more than four overlapping ribbons — the layered suite
  reaches five;
- ribbon centre lines were recovered by splitting the outline at its midpoint
  *by index*, which mispairs the two sides whenever they are sampled
  differently. On an arc-based ribbon, whose two sides have different radii,
  this displaced the recovered line by up to half the ribbon's breadth.

**Recommended wording.** Report comparable overlap to `d3-sankey-diagram` and
roughly threefold better than `d3-sankey`, and lead the section on the
crossings and speed results, which are strong and hold across both scales.

## New, not in the submission

**Layout speed.** Mean wall-clock time per dataset:

| Suite | d3-sankey | d3-sankey-diagram | flex-sankey |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| energy | 1 ms | 582 ms | **89 ms** |
| layered | 2 ms | 7 680 ms | **430 ms** |

`flex-sankey` is 6.5x faster than `d3-sankey-diagram` on the energy suite and
18x faster on the larger one, so the gap widens with size. `d3-sankey` is
faster than both, which is expected: it does no dummy-node routing and far less
optimisation, which is also why it ranks worst on crossings and overlap.

## Caveat

The comparison depends on parameterisation. `flex-sankey` was run with the
settings above while the comparison libraries used their defaults; different
choices shift the numbers. The crossings result is robust to this — it holds on
every dataset in both suites — whereas the overlap near-tie sits well within
the range that tuning can move.

## Real-world data (added after review)

Reviewer 1 asked for non-synthetic instances ("No real-world instances?",
manuscript p9). The benchmark now includes a third suite of one published
dataset: a 2050 UK energy scenario from the Department of Energy & Climate
Change via Tom Counsell, taken unmodified from the `d3-sankey` example that
uses it. Provenance is recorded in `benchmarks/real/PROVENANCE.md`.

It is deliberately the dataset `d3-sankey` itself ships as its showcase, so
the comparison cannot be said to rest on data chosen to suit this library.

### Shape

| | nodes | links | dummies | dummies/link |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| energy (synthetic) | 16–18 | 34–45 | 19–31 | 0.56–0.69 |
| layered (synthetic) | 52–54 | 133–140 | 67–72 | 0.50–0.51 |
| **real (UK 2050)** | **48** | **68** | **63** | **0.93** |

27 of its 68 links skip at least one layer and the longest spans six, so it
carries a heavier routing burden per link than either generated suite.

### Result

| library | crossings | overlap % | excess % | length | ms |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| d3-sankey | 134 | 19.92 | 22.27 | **12 697** | **1** |
| d3-sankey-diagram | 65 | 4.21 | 4.38 | 14 259 | 607 |
| **flex-sankey** | **56** | **3.65** | **3.76** | 14 642 | 120 |

Two things are worth stating plainly:

- This is the **only** suite where `flex-sankey` wins overlap outright. On the
  synthetic suites it ties `d3-sankey-diagram` (7.18 vs 7.29; 14.36 vs 14.37),
  a gap small enough to be parameterisation. Here it is 3.65 vs 4.21.
- `flex-sankey` draws the **longest** total link length of the three. The
  ribbons are smoother and overlap less, and they are not shorter. This should
  be said rather than omitted.

### A bug this exposed

`sankeyAddDummies` derived dummy keys as `source-target-N` and searched only
`N = 1..4`, throwing `dummy key heuristic exhausted` beyond that. A link
spanning *n* layers needs *n−1* dummies, so any link spanning more than five
layers failed outright.

Both synthetic suites cap spans at three (`LAYERED_SHAPE.maxSpan`), so nothing
in the benchmark had ever reached the limit. The real dataset has a
`Bio-conversion → Losses` link spanning six layers, which hit it immediately.

The cap served no purpose: `nodeMap` is finite, so the search terminates
regardless. It was removed. The library previously could not lay out a graph
with a link spanning more than five layers — a real limitation, not a
hypothetical one, and not one any synthetic test would have found.

## The non-overlap guarantee, stated precisely (added after review)

The submission said parallel ribbons "provably" cannot overlap, resting on the
fact that adjacent ribbons share a boundary curve exactly. A reviewer pointed
out that this does not follow: two ribbons can share a boundary and still have
that boundary cross a neighbouring one somewhere between the endpoints. The
objection is correct, and the claim needed splitting into what is proved and
what is measured.

**Proved.** Within a bundle — the ribbons joining one ordered pair of node
faces, abutting at both ends, which is what a packed run of dummy nodes
produces:

1. Adjacent ribbons share a boundary exactly. Both sides compute the same four
   control points, because the reference anchors depend on the node pair alone.
2. Every boundary has the same `y` profile. `P0`/`P1` carry the source height
   `α` and `P2`/`P3` the target height `β`, so the cubic's `y` component is
   `α·g(t) + β·(1-g(t))` with `g(t) = (1-t)²(1+2t)` — the same `g` for every
   boundary in the bundle, and `g'(t) = -6t(1-t) ≤ 0`, so it decreases
   monotonically. A boundary that starts and ends above another is above it
   throughout.
3. With `maxAngle: 0` the tangent scale is identically 1, so the whole bundle
   shares one `x` profile too, non-decreasing for `bias ≤ 1`. Equal `x` then
   means equal `t`, and (2) finishes it: consistently ordered ribbons cannot
   cross. Ribbons ordered one way at the source and the other way at the
   target do cross, as they must.

**Measured, not proved.** Above `maxAngle: 0` the tangent scale varies from
boundary to boundary, the shared `x` profile is lost and (3) no longer
follows. Sweeping bundle geometries, crossings do appear — but only where the
gap along the flow direction is a small fraction of the gap across it *and* a
single ribbon is wider than that gap. The layout stages do not produce that.
Across all 21 benchmark datasets at the default angle, every within-bundle
boundary crossing is an order inversion of the kind in (3), and there is none
between consistently ordered ribbons.

Both proved parts are now executable tests in `test/outline.test.js`.

### Two numbers the manuscript had wrong

- The default angle limit was reported as 30°. That is the default of the
  functions in `paths/outline.js`; `buildScene` — which is what actually draws
  a diagram — defaults to 20°, and the manuscript figures are drawn at 20°.
- Equation (1) was written with the offset term as `(d - p)`. The source end
  uses `δ - q_a` and the target end `δ + q_b`; the signs differ between the
  two ends and the single form hid that.
